## Core concept

The densest stage in the course. After the handshake the connection carries
length-prefixed messages — 4-byte length, 1-byte id, payload — and a fixed
opening dance: wait for `bitfield`, send `interested`, wait for `unchoke`, then
request.

**Choking** is the protocol's fairness mechanism: a peer refuses to serve you
until it decides to, and you cannot request anything before `unchoke`.

Then the split that surprises people: a piece is typically 256 KiB but a
`request` may ask for at most a **16 KiB block**, so one piece is many requests.
Sending them one at a time works and is slow; sending them all and matching
replies by `(index, begin)` is the design.

Finally, SHA-1 the assembled piece and compare against the hash from the
metainfo **before** writing anything. That check is the entire reason you can
download from strangers.

```
after the handshake, every message is framed:

 +-------------+------+--------------------+
 | length (4B) | id   | payload            |    length 0 = keep-alive
 +-------------+------+--------------------+

 ids: 0 choke  1 unchoke  2 interested  5 bitfield  6 request  7 piece

one piece is many requests:

  piece 0 (256 KiB)
 +--------+--------+--------+ ... +--------+
 | block  | block  | block  |     | block  |    16 KiB each
 +--------+--------+--------+     +--------+
  begin=0  16384    32768          (last one may be short)

 request: index | begin | length      piece: index | begin | data
                                              ^
                                     match replies by (index, begin) --
                                     they can arrive in any order
```

```
the opening sequence is fixed -- you may not request before unchoke:

   you                                   peer
    |  <handshake, 68 bytes>              |
    |------------------------------------>|
    |                       <handshake>   |
    |<------------------------------------|
    |                       bitfield (5)  |   which pieces it has
    |<------------------------------------|
    |  interested (2)                     |
    |------------------------------------>|
    |                       unchoke (1)   |   <- permission to ask
    |<------------------------------------|
    |  request (6) index,begin,length     |
    |------------------------------------>|
    |                       piece (7)     |
    |<------------------------------------|

choking is the fairness mechanism: a peer serves you when it decides to,
not when you ask.
```

## Go APIs

- `binary.BigEndian` for lengths and offsets; `io.ReadFull` per message.
- A buffer per piece, filled by offset.

## Hints

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>

The last piece and the last block are short — compute their sizes, do not
assume.
</details>

<details><summary>Approach</summary>

Get one block working before pipelining. The failure modes are much easier to
read that way.
</details>

<details><summary>Implementation</summary>

`request` carries index, begin and length as three 4-byte big-endian
integers; `piece` echoes index and begin before the data, which is how a
reply is matched to its request.
</details>
