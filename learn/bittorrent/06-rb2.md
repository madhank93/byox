## Core concept

The info hash is the **SHA-1 of the bencoded `info` dictionary** — of the
bytes, not of the meaning. So computing it means re-encoding what you decoded,
byte for byte: same key order, same integer forms, same raw strings. A decoder
that "helpfully" normalises anything produces a hash no peer recognises.

It identifies the torrent everywhere: in tracker requests, in the handshake, in
magnet links. Get it wrong and every stage from here fails with no useful
error.

## Go APIs

- An encoder that is the exact inverse of your decoder.
- `crypto/sha1`, printed with `encoding/hex`.

## Hints

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>

Easier alternative: record the byte range of the `info` value while decoding
and hash the original slice.
</details>

<details><summary>Approach</summary>

That slice trick sidesteps re-encoding entirely and is what a careful parser
does anyway.
</details>
