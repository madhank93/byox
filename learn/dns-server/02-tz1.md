## What this stage teaches

The 12-byte header is six 16-bit big-endian fields, and one of them — the flags
word — packs eight separate values into sixteen bits: QR, OPCODE, AA, TC, RD,
RA, Z and RCODE. Space was scarce and every byte counted, so DNS packs
aggressively.

Writing it is shifts and ORs:
`qr<<15 | opcode<<11 | aa<<10 | tc<<9 | rd<<8 | ra<<7 | z<<4 | rcode`.

## Go you'll reach for

- `binary.BigEndian.PutUint16` — network byte order, always.

## Hints

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>

The response copies the request's ID; a client uses it to match replies.
</details>

<details><summary>Approach</summary>

change individual bits, and a constant hides which.
</details>
