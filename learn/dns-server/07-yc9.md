## What this stage teaches

The cleverest and most dangerous part of the format. A length byte whose top
two bits are `11` is not a length — it is a **pointer**, and the remaining 14
bits are an offset from the start of the *message* where the rest of the name
lives. That is how a packet full of `mail.example.com` and `www.example.com`
stores `example.com` once.

Two consequences follow. Parsing a name needs the **whole message**, not the
slice you are standing in — so every name reader needs the full buffer plus an
offset. And a pointer can point anywhere, including backwards into itself: a
malicious packet can make a naive parser loop forever. Bound the jumps.

## Go you'll reach for

- `if b&0xC0 == 0xC0` to detect a pointer.
- `int(b&0x3F)<<8 | int(next)` for the 14-bit offset.
- A jump counter as a cheap loop guard.

## Hints

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>

After following a pointer, the name ends there — the caller's offset advances
past the two pointer bytes, not past the name it pointed at.
</details>

<details><summary>Approach</summary>

This is a real-world attack class, not a theoretical one. Treat every packet as
hostile input.
</details>
