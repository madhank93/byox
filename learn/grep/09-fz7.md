## What this stage teaches

The stage that makes this a backtracking engine. `+` is **greedy**: it takes as
many repetitions as it can, then — if the rest of the pattern fails — gives one
back and tries again. That "give one back and retry" *is* backtracking.

`a+ab` against `aaab` is the canonical demonstration: `a+` swallows all three
`a`s, `ab` has nothing left, so it retreats to two and succeeds.

## Go you'll reach for

- A count of how many repetitions matched, then a loop from that count
  downward, recursing on the remainder for each.

## Hints

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>

`+` requires at least one, so stop the retreat at 1.
</details>

<details><summary>Approach</summary>

Try longest first. Trying shortest first also finds *a* match, but not the one
a greedy engine is supposed to report.
</details>
