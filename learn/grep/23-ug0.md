## What this stage teaches

`a{2,4}` is the general form, and every other quantifier is now a special case
of it. Worth noticing what the bounds buy: they cap the backtracking, which is
why a bounded quantifier cannot blow up the way a nested unbounded one can.

## Go you'll reach for

- The retreat loop from `min(matched, max)` down to `min`.

## Hints

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>

Fewer than n repetitions is a failure, not a partial match.
</details>

<details><summary>Approach</summary>

Start from `min(matched, max)` and retreat to `min`. Fewer than `min`
repetitions is a failure, not a shorter match.
</details>
<details><summary>The API</summary>

Parse `{n,m}`, `{n,}` and `{n}` in one function — they differ only in which
parts are present.
</details>
