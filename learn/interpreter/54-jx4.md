## What this stage teaches

`and` mirrors `or`: return the left operand if it is falsey, otherwise the
right. It binds **tighter** than `or`, so `a or b and c` is `a or (b and c)` —
one more precedence layer between `or` and equality.

## Go you'll reach for

- An `and()` level called by `or()`.

## Hints

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>

</details>

<details><summary>Approach</summary>

</details>
