## What this stage teaches

The parser starts here, and with it the second phase of the pipeline:
characters became tokens, and tokens now become a **tree**. The `parse` command
prints that tree, which is why literals print as bare `true`, `false`, `nil`
rather than as tokens.

Keeping the phases apart is the discipline that carries the whole course: the
parser never looks at characters, and the printer never looks at tokens.

## Go you'll reach for

- An `Expr` interface and a `LiteralExpr` implementing it.
- A `Parser` struct holding the tokens and a position.

## Hints

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>

Three token types map to three literal values. The output is the value, not the
lexeme.
</details>

<details><summary>Approach</summary>

Give `Expr` a `String()` method now — every parsing stage prints its tree, and
the format is the same parenthesised form throughout.
</details>
