## What this stage teaches

Lox has one number type — a double — so `42` and `42.0` are the same value, and
the tester checks that the literal prints canonically: `42` has lexeme `42` and
literal `42.0`. Formatting a float back to text is a decision, not a given, and
this is where you make it.

## Go you'll reach for

- A digit loop, then a check for `.` followed by another digit.
- `strconv.ParseFloat`, and a formatter that keeps at least one decimal place.

## Hints

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>

`1234.5678` keeps its digits; `100` becomes `100.0`.
</details>

<details><summary>Approach</summary>

A trailing dot (`123.`) is not part of the number — the dot is its own token,
because Lox has no method-call syntax that would need it attached.
</details>

<details><summary>The API</summary>

`strconv.FormatFloat(v, 'f', -1, 64)` gives the shortest exact form; append
`.0` when the result has no decimal point, since the tokenizer prints `42` as
`42.0`.
</details>
