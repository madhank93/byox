## What this stage teaches

An expression followed by a semicolon is a statement that evaluates its
expression and **throws the value away**. That sounds pointless until you
remember side effects — a function call is the usual reason to write one.

It is also the parser's default: if a statement does not start with a keyword,
it is an expression statement.

## Go you'll reach for

- An `ExpressionStmt` wrapping an `Expr`.

## Hints

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>

`42;` on its own prints nothing. Only `print` produces output.
</details>

<details><summary>Approach</summary>

In `statement()`, check for `print` first and fall through to an expression
statement otherwise.
</details>
