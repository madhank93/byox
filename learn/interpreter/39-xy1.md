## What this stage teaches

The fourth command, `run`, and with it a new grammatical category. Until now
everything was an **expression** — it produces a value. `print` is a
**statement** — it produces an effect. Lox keeps them in separate hierarchies,
which is why a statement can contain an expression but not the reverse.

## Go you'll reach for

- A `Stmt` interface alongside `Expr`, and a `PrintStmt`.
- An `execute(stmt)` alongside `evaluate(expr)`.

## Hints

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>

`print "Hello";` — the semicolon is part of the statement, not the expression.
</details>

<details><summary>Approach</summary>

Reuse `stringify` for the printed form so `print 42;` shows `42`, not `42.0`.
</details>
