## What this stage teaches

A response with a body needs the receiver to know where the body ends, and in
HTTP/1.1 that is `Content-Length` in bytes. This is the stage where the framing
rule from the primer becomes concrete: header, blank line, then exactly that
many bytes.

## Go you'll reach for

- `strings.TrimPrefix(path, "/echo/")` to get the parameter.
- `len(body)` — bytes, and for ASCII that equals the character count.
- `fmt.Sprintf` to assemble the response.

## Hints

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>

Two headers are needed: `Content-Type: text/plain` and `Content-Length`.
</details>

<details><summary>Approach</summary>

Build the body string first, then the headers around it, so the length can never
drift out of sync with what you actually write.
</details>

<details><summary>The API</summary>

The full shape:
`HTTP/1.1 200 OK\r\nContent-Type: text/plain\r\nContent-Length: 3\r\n\r\nabc`
— note there is no CRLF after the body.
</details>
