## What this stage teaches

Three or more stages, which is where descriptor discipline stops being
forgiving. Each segment needs the previous read end as stdin and a fresh pipe
for its stdout, and the parent must close **every** end it still holds after
handing it to a child. One leaked descriptor anywhere in the chain and the
pipeline hangs.

## Go you'll reach for

- A loop carrying the previous read end forward, closing as it goes.

## Hints

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>

The last segment writes to the terminal, not to a pipe.
</details>

<details><summary>Approach</summary>

If two stages work and three hang, look for the read end of the first pipe
still open in the parent.
</details>

<details><summary>The API</summary>

Carry the previous segment's read end into the next iteration, and close
the old one once it has been handed over. A descriptor still open in the
parent is the difference between a pipeline that finishes and one that hangs.
</details>
