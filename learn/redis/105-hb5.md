## Core concept

Decoding a score back to a coordinate — and confronting that the encoding is
**lossy**. 26 bits per axis quantises the world into cells, so what comes back
is the centre of the cell the original point fell into, not the original point.
Round-tripping a coordinate gives you something very close and never identical,
which is why the tester compares with a tolerance.

## Go APIs

- De-interleave the even and odd bits back into two 26-bit integers.
- Scale back into degrees, then take the **midpoint** of the cell.

## Hints

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>

Compute the cell's low and high bounds and return `(low + high) / 2`.
</details>

<details><summary>Approach</summary>

Test `decode(encode(x)) ≈ x` for several coordinates. Systematic drift in one
direction means you are returning a cell edge instead of its centre.
</details>

<details><summary>Implementation</summary>

De-interleaving is `spreadBits` reversed: mask with `0x5555...`, then fold
the bits back together with shifts of 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16.
</details>
