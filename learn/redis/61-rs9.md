## What this stage teaches

Inside `MULTI`, commands are not executed — they are queued, and the client gets
`+QUEUED`. This inverts the dispatcher: instead of "parse then execute", it
becomes "parse, then decide whether to execute or store". That interception
point is the whole of transactions.

## Go you'll reach for

- An early branch in the dispatcher: if `inMulti` and the command isn't a
  control command, append to `queued` and reply `+QUEUED\r\n`.

## Hints

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>

`MULTI`, `EXEC` and `DISCARD` are not queued — they are acted on immediately.
</details>

<details><summary>Approach</summary>

Copy the argument slice when queueing. Reusing a buffer the parser overwrites is
a bug that shows up only with multiple queued commands.
</details>
