## Core concept

Your first RESP reply. `+PONG\r\n` is a *simple string*: a `+` type byte, the
text, and CRLF. Simple strings are for short, fixed, non-binary answers — status
words like `OK` and `PONG` — which is why they carry no length prefix.

You can pass this stage without parsing the request at all, and that is worth
noticing: the protocol is asymmetric, and reading is the harder half.

## Go APIs

- `conn.Read(buf)` to consume the incoming bytes (you may discard them here).
- `conn.Write([]byte("+PONG\r\n"))`.

## Hints

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>

Read something before replying. A client that never sees its bytes consumed can
stall on a full socket buffer.
</details>

<details><summary>Approach</summary>

Hardcoding the reply is fine at this stage — the next three stages will force
the real parser out of you, and building it before you need it usually produces
the wrong shape.
</details>
