## What this stage teaches

The core of the geo feature. Two coordinates become one sortable integer by
**interleaving their bits**: quantise longitude and latitude to 26 bits each
over their valid ranges, then interleave them — latitude in the even positions,
longitude in the odd — into a 52-bit geohash score.

Interleaving is what makes proximity work. Numbers that are close in the encoded
space are close on the map, because sharing a prefix of interleaved bits means
sharing a quadrant at every level of subdivision. That is what turns "find
places near me" into a score range query on a sorted set.

```
interleaving turns two coordinates into one sortable number:

  lat bits   a5 a4 a3 a2 a1 a0        (26 bits, quantised over +-85.05112878)
  lon bits   b5 b4 b3 b2 b1 b0        (26 bits, quantised over +-180)

  score      b5 a5 b4 a4 b3 a3 b2 a2 b1 a1 b0 a0    = 52 bits
             ^  ^
             |  latitude in the EVEN positions
             longitude in the ODD positions

why it works: sharing a prefix of the interleaved bits means sharing a
quadrant at every level of subdivision --

   +--------+--------+        each extra bit pair splits the box again,
   |  01    |   11   |        so "close in score" means "close on the map",
   +--------+--------+        which turns proximity search into a range query
   |  00    |   10   |
   +--------+--------+
```

## Go you'll reach for

- Quantise: `(coord - min) / (max - min) * (1 << 26)`, truncated.
- Spread each 26-bit value into alternating bits (a shift-and-mask ladder, or a
  plain bit loop) and combine with `lat | (lon << 1)`.
- `uint64`, converted to `float64` for the score.

## Hints

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>

Latitude's range is ±85.05112878, longitude's is ±180 — the quantisation
divisors differ.
</details>

<details><summary>Approach</summary>

Test the encoder against a known landmark's published score before building
anything on top. Every later geo stage inherits an error here silently.
</details>

<details><summary>The API</summary>

A 52-bit integer is exactly representable in a `float64` (which has 53 bits of
mantissa), which is why this scheme can hide inside a sorted set's score at all.
</details>
